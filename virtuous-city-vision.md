# Ending the Gaza War Must Restore Our Humanity

*The Virtuous City Vision · David Hanna Jr. · Unofficial, a private author's proposal · October 2026*

The Israeli-Palestinian conflict has long resembled a malformed bone: broken, poorly set, fractured again, and left to limp onward in dysfunction. With the Board of Peace established and Phase Two of Trump's 20-point plan underway, that hardened structure has briefly softened — and enough pieces of the post-war mosaic are now visible to paint a new architecture for Israel, Palestine, and the wider Middle East. The bone can be reset properly, or harden again into another frozen conflict that guarantees the next war.

The current trajectory has real, if fledgling, momentum in diplomacy, seed money, and technocratic planning. What is missing is the civilizational soul capable of sustaining what the Trump administration is attempting: bringing Peace to the Middle East.

The Trump administration deserves real credit for articulating a multi-billion-dollar economic vision to rebuild and reimagine Gaza as a regional economic hub. But any honest observer recognizes that their efforts represent only a fraction of the money, time, and collective will required — because this architecture must simultaneously restore Palestinian dignity and prosperity while recovering Israeli existential security, for two nations that prize self-determination above all else. Any architecture will inevitably step on the toes of both peoples in the Sisyphean task of ending this conflict, and the international community will be asked to shoulder disproportionate responsibility for the consequences.

The Virtuous City of Gaza is envisioned as more than a commercial or tourist center. It shall become a nucleus of intellectual, spiritual, and moral renewal — a reconciliation project between Western and Islamic civilizations, and a covenant that the Holy Land must never again become a battlefield for reciprocal genocide.

## The Telos of the Virtuous City of Gaza

[Abu Nasr Muhammad Al-Farabi's](https://youtube.com/watch?v=uZ30XUDnK-g) [Opinions of the People of the Virtuous City](https://www.amazon.com/Perfect-State-Abu-Nasr-al-Farabi/dp/1871031761) is more than an obscure utopian text preserved under the patronage of a bygone Abbasid Emir. It is the singular spiritual template for Gaza’s rebirth.

At its heart, the Virtuous City exists for one supreme purpose: to enable every inhabitant to attain true happiness (sa'ada) — the full actualization of human potential through intellectual perfection, moral virtue, civic cooperation, and the ultimate union with the divine, culminating in eternal felicity.  A city is truly virtuous only when its leaders, institutions, education, and shared life mutually support this collective flourishing in this world and the next, with each part of society functioning harmoniously like organs in a healthy body.

In this sense, the Virtuous City becomes a nucleus for a new social order and contract among Gaza’s future inhabitants, and the political and philosophical north star guiding reconstruction and nation-building after catastrophe.

The Virtuous City is an Arab-Islamic political and philosophical framework developed at the height of the Islamic world's engagement with Greek, Jewish, and Christian thought, where reason, revelation, ethics, and law existed in productive tension rather than outright opposition. That intellectual milieu produced shared advances in philosophy, medicine, mathematics, and jurisprudence. The Virtuous City is, in essence, an Islamic reinterpretation of Plato’s Republic, seeded with the soul of Medina, with the connection to the divine as the ultimate pathway to happiness.

For Palestinians, education has long functioned as a form of resistance: a way to preserve dignity, identity, and future orientation under occupation, displacement, and hopelessness. Gaza remains among the most educated societies in the Arab world relative to material conditions. The Virtuous City Vision elevates that instinct into the foundation of Palestinian rebuilding and national renewal.

Crucially, the Virtuous City’s emphasis on education, communal responsibility, wise and virtuous rulership, and collective flourishing also resonates with Jewish ethical traditions. It offers a shared moral substrate through which both peoples can recognize legitimate authority, justice, and the raison d'être of their polity.

Most importantly, the Virtuous City offers Gaza something it has never been allowed to have: a positive telos (goal). Gaza’s modern political identity has been defined almost entirely by resistance, survival, and siege/occupation. The Virtuous City redefines that horizon toward human flourishing through moral, intellectual, and spiritual development.

Without a clear telos, security architectures default to mere conflict management, and economic visions risk devolving into a façade of permanent aid dependency. With the Virtuous City as its north star, Gaza’s reconstruction becomes a civilizational project rather than a humanitarian holding pattern. Only once this foundation is established does it make sense to discuss disarmament, reconstruction, and a long-term security architecture.

The Virtuous City of Gaza draws on Al-Farabi's framework only for its telos — its sense of moral and spiritual purpose. It is intentionally scoped to define only what the polity is for — not how it is governed. It doesn't call for a philosopher-king, a clerical guardian, or the creation of any kind of elitist Platonic ruling class. The actual mechanics of governance would be those of modern democracy: constitutional law, civic accountability, elected leaders who serve at the public's pleasure and can be voted out.

A new telos for Gaza without a supporting architecture is just an aspiration. The current institutional framework, the Board of Peace, isn't yet able to bear its weight. That can be fixed.

## The Board of Peace: Three Discordances and a Coalition

President Trump has articulated a broad vision for the Board of Peace as a new international peacemaking body capable of addressing major global conflicts. However, in its current form, there are three distinct discordances that will harm its ability to end the Israel-Hamas War.

1. First, there is a mismatch between the Board of Peace as it was initially framed, a tighter Gaza-first mandate, and what the Charter ultimately implies, a broader peacemaking body with global scope, causing concern for many potential member states.
2. Relatedly, there is a discordance between the Board’s current composition and the minimum viable coalition that would actually be required to resolve the Israeli–Palestinian conflict. Key European players, like the UK, France, Germany, and the Vatican, refuse to join the BoP, while only a subset of its current Members and Observers are likely to be materially significant to the Israeli-Palestinian conflict.
3. Third and finally, the Board of Peace’s original conception was to resolve the Gaza war — an instance of a century-old conflict in the heart of the Holy Land. It absolutely requires a cultural, moral, and ideological foundation that matches the soul of the Holy Land and its people. As currently structured, it primarily serves as a transactional, administrative, and diplomatic mechanism. Without an explicit philosophical and reconciliation substrate, it doesn't do justice to the human complexity of the conflict.

These discordances do not require abandoning the Board of Peace. They require clarifying their structural role. The Board should not attempt to administer Gaza indefinitely beyond its authorized two-year mandate. It should instead function as an incubator — convening, authorizing, and stabilizing a focused coalition of the willing tailored specifically to the Gaza conflict, then spinning that coalition off into an independent guarantor body.

Accordingly, and consistent with Article 3 of the Board of Peace Charter, the appropriate next step is the formation of a subsidiary coalition of the willing — the Coalition for Canaan — a focused, semi-independent guarantor bloc incubated and initially shepherded by the Board.

The Coalition for Canaan would focus specifically on the broader Israeli–Palestinian conflict, while providing a structural pathway for states reluctant to join the Board as currently conceived — bridging President Trump’s expansive global vision with partners who prefer a narrower, conflict-specific mandate.

### The Five-Point mandate for the Coalition for Canaan

1. Moral: Break the cycle of genocide. Israelis widely view the October 7 attack as a genocidal massacre; Palestinians widely view the subsequent Gaza war as genocide. The Coalition commits to formally acknowledging these perceptions and preventing their recurrence.
2. Political: Using the Trump Plan to end the Gaza war as a basis, establish a generational, time-bound pathway to Palestinian self-governance over a 20 to 30-year horizon, with the Coalition responsible for laying the political, security, and institutional conditions necessary for a Palestinian polity to serve Israel’s long-term security interests. The two-state formula will not be a panacea for the Holy Land. Still, a credible exit plan for Palestinian self-governance is necessary for a stable interim regional order. Under the 20-point plan, the PA has a pathway to return to Gaza within this timeframe, so we must plan for success.
3. Humanitarian. Implement the Trump administration’s 20-point plan to stabilize Gaza and address its humanitarian catastrophe, guarantee Palestinian freedom of movement, alongside rebuilding and protecting housing, education, and healthcare infrastructure for those who choose to stay.
4. Civilizational. Heal the Western–Arab and Islamic divide that this conflict continues to aggravate, and which economic coupling alone cannot resolve.
5. Spiritual. Re-covenant the sanctity of peace in the Holy Land. Religious reconciliation frameworks will be developed, and Holy sites will be protected under negotiated covenants grounded in religious legitimacy, helping to restore the moral gravity that once restrained mass violence in the Holy Land.

Initial core members of the Coalition for Canaan would include Israel, Palestine (PA), and the core mediator states, the United States, Egypt, Turkey, and Qatar. Additional members would likely include the UAE, Jordan, Saudi Arabia, Norway, the UK, and selected EU member states such as the Vatican, Germany, France, Italy, and the Netherlands. Major contributors to the ISF, like Indonesia, Morocco, Kazakhstan, Kosovo, and Albania, would also be core members of the Coalition.

These nations would provide the core economic, security, humanitarian, and reconstruction guarantees underlying the architecture, while normal Board of Peace member states and observers provide ancillary support.

Membership would expand or contract as required to implement the Virtuous City Vision. The proposed lifespan of the Coalition is roughly one generation (30 Years), structured as an initial 10-year phase followed by two optional extension phases to fully realize the Vision’s political, security, and reconciliation frameworks.

Properly structured, this model is repeatable. The Board of Peace can incubate focused guarantor coalitions wherever hardened territorial disputes and civilizational fault lines threaten prolonged instability — whether in Russia-Ukraine, Armenia–Azerbaijan, Cambodia–Thailand, the DRC–Rwanda conflict, India–Pakistan, or future fault-lines not yet erupted. The strength of the model lies in its ability to stand up conflict-specific architectures, stabilize them, and allow them to mature into durable, self-sustaining frameworks for security.

## The Generational Timeline of the Virtuous City Vision

The Virtuous City Vision operates on a generational timescale of roughly 30 years, structured in three phases.

The first decade is focused on stabilization: demilitarize Gaza, stand up the Coalition of the Willing, establish the transitional government, begin reconstruction, and lay the institutional foundations for Palestinian self-governance.

The second decade shifts toward integration: deepening Palestinian self-governance, expanding economic ties with Israel and the broader region, advancing the reconciliation and educational frameworks, and continuing normalization under the Abraham Accords.

The final phase aims for full maturity: mutual state recognition, voluntary confederation between Israel, Palestine, and the wider Middle East, complete Israeli military withdrawal, and the full implementation of the reconciliation frameworks seeded in the first two decades.

This is by no means a utopian timeline. It represents a generational commitment to doing what no ceasefire, no peace deal, and no economic package has ever attempted: building something durable enough to endure and outlast the next crisis in the Middle East.

With a defined political horizon and substrate, a long-term demilitarization framework for the Strip becomes viable. Nobody disarms without a future.

## Demilitarization and the Political Transition of Resistance Factions

The Virtuous City Vision adopts the disarmament, security-transfer, verification, and Israeli-withdrawal arrangements negotiated under the Board of Peace process as its operational mechanism. It does not prescribe a competing framework. Instead, it sets out the principles those mechanisms must serve, beginning with One Authority, One Law, One Weapon under legitimate Palestinian governance.

Demilitarization must be inseparable from a credible political horizon. The obligation to disarm is reciprocal and conditioned on the agreed withdrawal and political transition; it is not unilateral surrender. Any agreement must recognize the Palestinian people's right to self-defense against indefinite occupation as a foundational principle. Demilitarization does not extinguish that right, and the settlement is legitimate only insofar as it honors that right through withdrawal and sovereignty. A demilitarized people under continuing occupation is not at peace but simply defenseless, and this framework does not recognize such an arrangement as a settlement.

This is a matter of principle as much as pragmatism. In Axis Rule in Occupied Europe, Raphael Lemkin identified genocide as a technique of occupation. He described it as a way for an occupying power to secure in peace what it failed to win in war, carried out through coordinated political, economic, social, and cultural measures that dismantle the national pattern of the occupied and impose that of the occupier, and not only through mass killing. Indefinite occupation is therefore no morally neutral substitute for war: it can undo a people without a shot being fired. A framework that disarms Palestinians while leaving permanent external domination in place would reproduce the conditions that sustain resistance and ultimately destabilize the settlement.

The trade is not easy. Armed factions are asked to surrender their final coercive lever against occupation because the alternative has already been tried, at enormous cost, and has not ended the occupation either. Factions relinquish independent coercive power as occupation recedes. In return, the settlement provides phased Israeli withdrawal, credible regional and international security guarantees, and an enforceable pathway to self-determination and statehood. Responsibility for security passes from independent factions to legitimate Palestinian institutions under Palestinian authority. Egypt serves as the principal regional security anchor, and the Coalition for Canaan sustains the broader political and diplomatic framework beyond the Board of Peace process.

Armed resistance can only be durably displaced by a political system offering legitimate avenues for representation and national self-determination. Former armed factions should therefore have a pathway into ordinary political life. That pathway is contingent on verified relinquishment of independent coercive capacity and adherence to the Coalition for Canaan's Five-Point Mandate, the moral-political order governing the transition. Its five commitments are non-recurrence, Palestinian self-governance, humanitarian renewal, regional coexistence, and the sanctity of Israeli and Palestinian life, together with the government's monopoly on legitimate force. This is a common rule of the transitional order, not a bespoke punishment for Hamas. Factions may survive as political movements only by becoming genuinely political, which means operating through civilian institutions and accepting pluralist competition. Material violations may result in proportionate political restrictions under Palestinian law and the transitional framework.

Individuals who comply with demobilization should have meaningful pathways into civilian life. These include amnesty where appropriate, employment, education, retirement, social support, and safe relocation where credible security risks exist. They also include direct participation in reconstruction through the Palestinian Labor Movement and associated programs. The goal is demobilization without social destruction: ending armed organizations without creating a permanently excluded class of former fighters vulnerable to radicalization, and building an order just and durable enough that renewed militancy no longer seems the only path left.

## Palestinian Freedom of Movement

Any credible postwar framework must confront a basic moral reality: no people should be forcibly confined to a devastated enclave after mass destruction. Ending cycles of genocidal violence requires not only protection for those who remain, but a genuine choice for those who wish to leave.

In line with its humanitarian mandate, the Coalition will negotiate, coordinate, and ensure the availability of pathways for asylum, temporary evacuation, and voluntary relocation for Gazans during the reconstruction period. These measures will be debated transparently and implemented through binding commitments by coalition member states, not left to ad hoc charity or discretionary border policies.

Temporary evacuation pathways will prioritize civilians unable to safely remain during early reconstruction, including the injured, the chronically ill, families with children, and individuals at heightened risk of retaliation or political violence. Evacuation is voluntary, protective, and reversible, with guaranteed best-effort rights of return to Gaza once conditions permit.

In parallel, asylum options will be made available to Gazans seeking permanent resettlement abroad, including access to legal resources, employment, education, and family reunification, negotiated directly with coalition states and monitored by the Board of Peace. This framework will also extend, on a case-by-case basis, to former members of resistance factions who have renounced armed violence, complied with demobilization requirements, and face credible threats to their safety as a result of the transition process. Such provisions are designed to reduce incentives for spoilers, enable demobilization, and prevent cycles of retaliatory violence.

## Trump's Economic Vision, Palestinian Labor, and Arab Reconstruction

The Trump economic vision emphasizes employment, reconstruction, and integration. To support demilitarization and make Gaza’s recovery viable and legitimate, the Board and Coalition should foster a non-partisan Palestinian Labor Movement whose primary task is to absorb, preserve, and redeploy displaced Palestinian human capital.

Gaza must be rebuilt by Palestinians themselves: centering Palestinian labor agency is the clearest check against reconstruction that erodes local ownership and political autonomy. Building on existing Palestinian labor institutions, this unity movement would operate outside factional politics during the transition and channel the skills of workers, engineers, teachers, healthcare professionals, civil servants, and demobilized fighters who renounce armed violence into reconstruction, using the Palestinian Authority’s reconstruction program as its baseline.

The United States must play a central role in financing and supporting Gaza’s economic revitalization. To anchor this effort, I propose a Compact of Free Association-style agreement between the United States and the Palestinian Authority. This proto-compact would provide a legal basis for the American reconstruction vision for Gaza, provide a pathway to integrate Gaza into global markets while preserving Palestinian agency, dignity, and self-determination, and retain the option to evolve into a full compact upon formal recognition of Palestinian statehood.

Without Arab capital, Gaza will not be rebuilt. At least one Arab state should be designated as the primary Reconstruction Custodian for the Gaza Strip, responsible for partnering with the transitional government to lead fundraising and investment by the Arab and Islamic world, in coordination with the Coalition and the BoP. The custodian would serve as the Arab world’s point woman for sustaining Gaza’s revival and as an advocate for full reconstruction within the wider coalition architecture.

The United Arab Emirates is currently the most suitable candidate, given its humanitarian contributions to Gaza and its diplomatic relations with the key actors, including the United States, Egypt, and Israel. Saudi Arabia could also fill this role, and doing so could create a pathway toward normalization tied directly to the broader Israeli-Palestinian process. The choice carries major implications for the future regional order.

A central financing pillar of the Virtuous City Vision should be the redirection of a portion Iranian frozen assets toward the Gaza Reconstruction/Virtuous City Trust Fund, matched by members of the Board of Peace. These assets roughly align in scale with the cost of rebuilding Gaza and represent one of the few existing capital pools capable of underwriting reconstruction at the necessary scale. Such a redirection could emerge within a broader diplomatic framework focused on ending the 2026 Iran War.

## The West Bank: Stabilization, Territorial Integrity, and Arab-Israeli Normalization

To preserve a viable pathway toward Palestinian self-governance, uphold the Coalition’s moral and political mandate, and enable deeper normalization with Syria and Lebanon, the Board of Peace shall establish a Holy Land Trust for the occupied West Bank, designed from inception as a framework expandable, as conditions permit, to other Israeli-occupied territories within a broader regional settlement framework.

The Trust shall operate two parallel, without-prejudice ledgers: first, internationally funded non-development easements over a defined map of contiguity-critical parcels; and second, use payments from Israel, held in escrow, over the registered footprint of existing settlements and outposts. Empty land shall remain empty. Built land shall remain limited to its registered footprint. Neither ledger shall transfer title, recognize annexation, or determine final borders. Both ledgers shall convert into final-status instruments only upon a negotiated settlement.

This is extremely critical as Members of the current Israeli governing coalition, like Itamar Ben-Gvir and [Bezalel Smotrich](https://hashiloach.org.il/israels-decisive-plan/), have articulated visions of settlement in the Occupied West Bank that result in the national destruction of the Palestinian nation.

It is worth recalling Raphael Lemkin’s original conception of genocide, which he described as unfolding in two phases: first, the destruction of the national pattern of the oppressed group; second, the imposition of the national pattern of the oppressor—a dynamic that is actively occurring in the West Bank. The world cannot let this occur, if only to prevent the Jewish soul from being burdened with the crime of genocidal erasure of another people.

Conversely, Palestinian reluctance must not become an obstacle to adopting this framework. The Palestinian Authority's current tactic of encouraging settlement in Area C of the West Bank as a means of resistance will find limited success under occupation. Palestinian leadership must exercise wisdom so that any bridging mechanism does not become de facto permanent.

Finally, the feasibility of pilot projects introducing the International Stabilization Force into the West Bank must be explored as part of a pathway toward the future security architecture of a demilitarized Palestinian state. Manpower constraints within the Israeli army have become a persistent feature of Israeli politics. In addition, the issue of rogue elements within the IDF turning a blind eye to—or even facilitating or participating in—settler attacks against Palestinians has long remained unresolved. The internationalization of the Israeli–Palestinian conflict, and the unprecedented international willpower to provide manpower for both Israeli and Palestinian security, must not go to waste due to short-term political calculations.

This is a pragmatic compromise to keep the two-state horizon on its deathbed rather than consigning it to the dustbin of history, preserving territorial compromise as an option for any future political settlement. Because the West Bank remains the linchpin of broader Arab–Israeli normalization, the Board of Peace should pursue these initiatives under the Abraham Accords framework.

## Philosophy, Education, and Rehumanization in the Land of Canaan

No postwar framework can succeed without confronting the philosophical and educational drivers that sustain this conflict. Security arrangements and economic reconstruction cannot hold if the underlying civilizational logic remains broken: if each nation remains trapped in narratives that render the Other as illegitimate, subhuman, or eternal enemy.

This is where Point 18 of the 20-point plan must be operationalized and institutionalized into durable curricula, civic programs, and shared reference points that future educators, scholars, clerics, and civic leaders can build on. This plan has no illusions about hardened attitudes, grief, rage, or the current emotional weather on either side. The purpose here is definition, not conclusion: to name the missing frameworks so that future generations of leaders can build them into something real.

Five reconciliation frameworks sit at the heart of the Virtuous City Vision:

**1) The Telos of the Virtuous City of Gaza.**
This is the future Palestinians must be able to operationalize—and the future Israelis must be able to see—if reconstruction, demilitarization, and coexistence are to be politically survivable. Gaza’s telos cannot merely be the absence of rockets. It must be the emergence of a society oriented toward education, civic virtue, and human flourishing. That is the kind of Gaza that becomes a security gain rather than a strategic risk.

**2) The Five-Point Mandate of the Coalition for Canaan.**
This is the geopolitical philosophy governing the Architecture: reciprocal obligations, grounded in geopolitical reality. It defines constraints Israel must accept for long-term regional legitimacy, and guarantees Palestinians must see to rationally engage with demilitarization, reform, and political transition. This is explicitly inspired by Ben-Gurion’s early vision of Israel and its sister Arab state as part of an Arab regional Federation.

Together, those two frameworks establish the civilizational and political conditions under which reconstruction and normalization become mutually intelligible rather than mutually threatening.

**3–5) Three educational and rehumanization frameworks**—to be jointly supported by Israel, Palestine, and the broader Coalition for Canaan—are proposed to give this civilizational mandate concrete form across generations.

These frameworks are not designed to erase national narratives. The purpose here is to define: to name and describe these traditions so that future leaders and educators, scholars can develop them into full curricula and reconciliation frameworks during the generational timescale of the Vision.

They are:

1. Shared Canaanite ethnogenesis: an anthropological rehumanization framework that acknowledges the shared ethnogenesis of the Israeli and Palestinian nations, rooted in the Land of Canaan, while recognizing divergence, admixture, and other complexities without adjudicating modern indigeneity, sovereignty, religion, or national identity. This lineage is highly contested in both nations, and it is presented with the explicit acknowledgement that contention over claims of indigeneity and sovereignty will not be eliminated.
2. The Formal Establishment of the Judeo-Islamic Tradition: formalizing the shared philosophical, theological, and intellectual heritage of Jews and Muslims: the very milieu in which the Virtuous City emerged, and a buried civilizational overlap that can be taught without forcing theological merger or political surrender.
3. The Formal Establishment of the Broader Abrahamic Tradition: consolidating interfaith initiatives into a coherent framework: building shared educational programs, and establishing covenants of coexistence, restraint, and mutual recognition among Jews, Muslims, and Christians—especially around contested holy sites, where sanctity must once again become covenant.

Religion-based peacemaking is not without direct precedent, most notably the dialogue pursued by [West Bank Settler Rabbi Menachem Froman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menachem_Froman) and Hamas Founder and Spiritual Leader [Ahmed Yassin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Yassin), two very unlikely souls who sought to ground conflict resolution through both Halakha and Sharia. Their effort did not succeed politically, but it perhaps offers the best prototype of the type of formal religious dialogue necessary to bring peace to the Holy Land.

The formalization of the Judeo-Islamic and the Abrahamic Traditions should also be situated within the broader framework of the Abraham Accords, extending normalization beyond diplomatic recognition into structured civilizational, educational, and religious reconciliation architecture.

Taken together, these five frameworks constitute the moral, educational, and civilizational architecture intended to move the conflict beyond its zero-sum logic and toward a durable, enforceable peace that operates at the spiritual level.

## The House of Wisdom and Peace

Finally, securing commitments to establish a House of Wisdom and Peace must be the Board of Peace’s keystone initiative—not a cultural side-project or an NGO vanity mission, but the intellectual anchor of the Virtuous City Vision.

Historically, the original House of Wisdom was a crossroads where the Islamic and Western worlds met through philosophy, science, and medicine. Modern discourse has flattened the relationship between Islam and the West into a clash of civilizations revolving around oil, geopolitics, and resentment, as if the only meaningful tie between the West and the Islamic world has been extraction, domination, or cultural friction.

There exists a deep intellectual, cultural, and spiritual linkage between these civilizations. Their relationship has always possessed a yin-and-yang character: for every era of conquest and violence, there was an equal and opposite force of transmission, synthesis, and mutual enrichment. The House of Wisdom represented the light within that historical collision, proving that civilizations can grow through contact rather than just consume one another.

This is where Truth and Reconciliation work must actually live. The House of Wisdom and Peace should be the originating institution for the educational and interfaith reconciliation frameworks of the Virtuous City Vision: where scholars, clerics, educators, and civic leaders can formally develop the curricula, covenants, and shared moral language needed to seed peace across generations. Without an intellectual engine that can produce and legitimize reconciliation frameworks, Israelis and Palestinians will relapse into internecine conflict.

Building a House of Wisdom and Peace in Gaza is not an anachronism. It is a wager that civilizational collision can once again leave a constructive mark upon holy ground long scarred by conflict. If such an institution cannot rise from the ashes of Gaza, then the Board of Peace will never be more than a vanity project. If it does, the Board of Peace would have earned its name and place in the annals of history.

## What the Fuck Was All of This Destruction For?

Anyone who has lived through these past three years has had to ask themselves one unavoidable question:

What the fuck was all this for?

Between the claims of victory on both sides, we have witnessed the darkest moments in the Israeli–Palestinian conflict by far. More than ever before, the trajectory on both sides resembles nothing less than full national erasure.

Nevertheless, we are living through an amorphous moment — a rare phase when a conflict that has ossified for generations briefly loses its fixed shape. Israelis and Palestinians alike must seize it. There is one point of consensus that cuts across both nations, regardless of ideology: the status quo is unacceptable.

Like it or not, Donald Trump is uniquely positioned to make unprecedented progress in the Israeli-Palestinian conflict. He has the political legitimacy, institutional leverage, and force of personality to impose a decisive end to the war and force a new vision into place.

Hamas and the Palestinian people stand at the most critical junction in their national movement. Palestinian steadfastness has always been about remaining on the land under overwhelming pressure.

Yet the reality is apocalyptic: Gaza has been reduced to rubble, its prospects for reconstruction are slim to none, and the Palestinian national project remains on its deathbed, despite unprecedented popular support for its liberation movement.

For Hamas and its members, the question must be answered: What's the worth of ruling over nothing but rubble? The international community will not finance the rebuilding of a territory governed by a people who are structurally committed to another catastrophe.

The Virtuous City Vision offers a way to affirm the Arab-Islamic nature of the Gaza Strip while engaging the West without capitulation or secular erasure. A movement that claims to defend Arab-Islamic identity should recognize that this framework is directly inspired by its deepest, most neglected inheritance. If Hamas seeks a future beyond governing ruins, endorsing the Virtuous City Vision is the most coherent path available to transform resistance into political survival, dignity, and renewal.

One should hold no illusions about the so-called "two-state solution": In every version ever proposed, a Palestinian “state” resembles neither a sovereign state nor a clean solution. It is better understood as an autonomy framework with constrained sovereignty, foreign dependencies, and permanent infringements on security. Nor is it realistic to believe that a century of trauma, dispossession, and mutual fear would dissolve simply because borders are redrawn, or flags are raised over more territory.

Yet abandoning it entirely would be a strategic error. However imperfect, a credible pathway toward Palestinian self-governance remains the only long-term political horizon capable of anchoring Israel's regional integration, stabilizing the Holy Land, and reducing the perpetual existential insecurity that now defines both Jewish and Palestinian life.

When the world moved to recognize Israel through the UN process, and by extension accepted the war and displacement of the Arab population that accompanied the end of Mandatory Palestine, it was not doing so with the intention of creating a prototype of modern ultra-nationalism. Whatever one thinks of the events leading up to 1948, the moral wager was that Israel would become a legitimate state for the safety and security of the Jewish people, and not a messianic engine of hegemony that hardens the region and world against its trajectory.

The Virtuous City Vision, while centered on Gaza, is an attempt to revive and modernize the alternative trajectory embedded in Israel’s own founding imagination, including Ben-Gurion’s openness to regional federation and binational or confederal horizons with the Arabs.

What is proposed here does not ask Israelis or Palestinians to pretend that history can be undone, identities dissolved, or grievances erased. It asks only that the war end decisively. Periodic wars punctuated by brief ceasefires are morally and strategically unacceptable.

If there is any unifying truth left after these three years of devastation, it is this: the planetary devastation that we have all borne in our nervous systems must never occur again.
